Every planet in this game carries four numbers that decide what flying there is
like: how hard gravity pulls, how thick the air is, how hard the wind blows,
and whether there is a sea to land in. The game shows you none of them.

They are all in its files, and they vary far more than the scenery suggests.
Basalt's gravity is nearly five times Helirion's. Baobara's wind is fifty-four
times the Moon's. A ship that is comfortable on Earth is not a ship that
survives everywhere.

This is the whole system, ordered from lightest to heaviest, with Earth marked
as the reference at gravity 10.0.

## The low-gravity worlds

**Helirion** — gravity **3.9**, air 0.72, wind 20, no sea. Station:
Bloomreach.

The easiest place in the system to fly. Thirty-nine per cent of Earth's pull
with thicker air than Ashbelt or Aundara, and almost no weather. It is also the
smallest body in the game by a wide margin — a radius of 36,220, less than a
fifteenth of Earth's — with the deepest atmosphere relative to its size,
reaching 7.3% of its radius above the surface. Underpowered ships that cannot
leave anywhere else can leave here.

**Moon** — gravity **4.1**, air 0.50, wind 8, **has a sea**. Station:
Moonstep.

Half of Earth's air, 41% of its gravity, and wind of 8 at force 0.40 — mild
enough to ignore, in the thickest air of any low-gravity body except Helirion.
The Moon is where the game expects you to make your first mistakes, and its
numbers say so.

**Kovo** — gravity **4.9**, air **0.06**, wind **0**, no sea. **No station.**

Effectively a vacuum. Six per cent air is the thinnest atmosphere in the system
by a factor of three, and the wind is flat zero — the only body where it is.
There is nowhere to dock, either. Kovo is a place you visit self-sufficiently
and leave, and anything that needs air to work will not work here.

## The moderate worlds

**Tenebra** — gravity **6.5**, air 0.62, wind **180**, **has a sea**. **No
station.**

Read the wind figure twice. Tenebra has the **highest wind force in the
game — 10.0** — at a speed of 180, and no station to shelter at. It also has
the strangest atmosphere in the files: its air band runs from 0.9627 to 0.9770
of the planet's radius, entirely _below_ the nominal surface. Whatever that
means in the terrain, it is unique to Tenebra, and it is worth arriving
prepared for the game not to behave the way the other fourteen taught you.

**Rimshell** — gravity **6.8**, air **0.21**, wind 5, no sea. Station:
Altimirror.

Near-calm — wind force 0.25, the second-lowest in the game — but the second
thinnest air, at 21%. A quiet place to fly with very little for a fan to bite
on. It has the tallest atmosphere of any planet as a fraction of its radius
after Helirion, reaching 6.5% above the surface, so the thin air is at least
spread out.

**Ashbelt** — gravity **7.3**, air 0.39, wind 23, **has a sea**. Station:
Emberline.

Unremarkable gravity and weather over a shallow atmosphere: the air band is
only 1.1% of the radius thick, one of the tightest in the game. The margin
between "in the atmosphere" and "out of it" is narrow here.

**Aundara** — gravity **7.8**, air 0.44, wind 38, **has a sea**. Stations:
Unda Prime, Teal Horizon.

The **thinnest atmospheric band in the system** — 0.62% of the radius — over a
large planet. Aundara is where an altimeter and an atmometer stop being
optional, because the transition happens fast. Two stations, which helps.

## Around Earth's weight

**Outcast** — gravity **8.5**, air 0.58, wind **165**, no sea. Stations:
Crownleaf, Icevine Valley.

Wind speed 165 at force 8.0, and the only planet in the game with **fixed
rotation** — the wind does not turn. That is either a permanent hazard or a
permanent free ride, depending on which way you are going, and it is the one
place where planning a route around the weather actually works.

**Goldtwin** — gravity **9.0**, air 0.49, wind 18, no sea. Station:
Triplepine.

Mild in every direction. One of the two Twins, and the tamer one.

**Ascensia** — gravity **9.4**, air 0.62, wind 36, no sea. Station: Jura
Ascenda.

Close to Earth on every axis except the sea. Its atmosphere starts _above_ the
surface — the air band runs from 1.003 of the radius, so the lowest ground has
no air over it at all.

**Earth** — gravity **10.0**, air 0.70, wind 30, **has a sea**. Stations:
Headquarters, Tutorial.

The reference. Everything you learn here is calibrated to these numbers.

## The heavy worlds

**Pinktwin** — gravity **10.6**, air 0.50, wind 10, no sea. Station: Palehorn.

The other Twin, and heavier and calmer than Goldtwin: 10.6 gravity against 9.0,
wind 10 against 18, in exactly half the air. Identical numbers to the Moon for
air and atmospheric band, at two and a half times the gravity.

**CoronaSilva** — gravity **10.7**, air **0.83**, wind 21, **has a sea**.
Station: Mudpit.

The **thickest atmosphere in the game** at 0.83, and the deepest air band of
any planet at Earth's weight or above. Heavy, but the air is working for you —
this is the best place in the system for anything that pushes against air
rather than reaction mass.

**Baobara** — gravity **11.4**, air 0.75, wind **270**, no sea. Stations:
Golden Grass, Void Watcher.

The largest body in the game — radius 1,120,000, twice Earth's — with the
**fastest wind in the game at 270** and, uniquely, an **always-on wind force of
4.5**. Every other planet's wind gusts; Baobara's has a floor. A ship parked on
Baobara is being pushed the whole time it sits there. Combined with 11.4
gravity, this is the hardest environment in the game to _hold station_ in,
which is a different problem from Basalt's.

**Basalt** — gravity **18.4**, air 0.67, wind 11, no sea. Stations: B02, Candy
Vein.

**1.84 times Earth's gravity**, and by a long way the hardest ascent in the
game. The nearest rival is Baobara at 11.4. The wind is almost nothing —
force 0.55 — which is the only mercy on offer. If a ship is going to be
underpowered anywhere, it will be here, and the arithmetic for that is in
[how to go up](/guides/how-to-go-up).

## What to read out of this

**Gravity decides whether you can leave.** Basalt at 18.4 needs nearly five
times the lift of Helirion at 3.9 for the same ship. Nothing else in the
numbers matters if this one defeats you.

**Air decides which thrusters are worth carrying.** CoronaSilva at 0.83 and
Kovo at 0.06 are different games. The files do not record which parts need
atmosphere to function — only what they weigh and what they push — so the air
figure is the warning, not the answer.

**Wind decides whether you can stop.** Baobara at 270 with a 4.5 floor,
Tenebra at force 10.0, and Outcast at 165 with fixed rotation are the three
that will move a parked ship. Everywhere else, wind force is under 2.0 and
mostly under 1.1.

**The sea is a landing option.** Six planets have one: Moon, Tenebra, Ashbelt,
Aundara, Earth, CoronaSilva. Every part in the game carries buoyancy data, so
water is a place a ship can end up floating rather than broken.

**Two planets have no station.** Kovo and Tenebra. Whatever you need there, you
bring — and both are awkward in other ways too, which is unlikely to be a
coincidence.

## Where the numbers come from

Every figure on this page is read out of the game's own universe data and shown
on each body's page in [Planets](/planets), alongside a globe rendered from its
real terrain. Air is the game's own density value from 0 to 1; the atmospheric
band is given as a fraction of the planet's radius; wind speed and force are
the game's two separate figures, and they do not move together. If a patch
changes the system, the planet pages change with it and this guide is what goes
stale.
